## Background
Foundation models are rapidly becoming distributed intelligence systems. In deep learning, we use distributed optimization to train a neural network over physically or virtually distributed computation workers to improve the training speed, performance, and sometimes privacy preservance. As a result, we can apply distribtued optimization to train a base LLM. On the other hand, with a trained LLM serving for the agent, multi-agent reaonsing explore that how we can let multi-agent collaborate each other only at the inference level to effectively resolve the questions. We can see that for real-world products, we are beginning to explore the opportunities in from distributed optimization and multi-agent collaboration 

Therefore, a natural question is what is the gap between developing multi-agent reaosning to distributed optimization? Why do we need to adapt from distributed optimization to multi-agent and how we can heuristclly apply the ideas from distributed optimization into multi-agent reaosning?

### Distributed Optimization
Over the past decade, we shift from conventional training and inference of neural networks on a single machine to distributed training and inference as the scale of model and data continues to grow. We let multiple computational workers collaboratively to optimize objectives through local computation, communication, and aggregation. Today, distributed optimziation is a part of the infrastructure to train a sucessful LLM.

In general, we can have a canonical formulation where our objective is to 

$$
\min_{\theta} F(\theta)=\min_{\theta} p_i f_i(\theta)
$$

where $\theta$ is the neural network we want to distributedly optimize. We use $f_i(\theta)$ to represetn the local objective on each computation worker $i$. Then, we can have different forms of $p_i$ where they can present the important of each local objective (e.g. used for finding pareto optimal) or we can design them as $p_i=\frac{\lvert d_i \rvert}{\sum_j \lvert d_j \rvert}$ meaning the propotion of samples ($d_i$) on each devices. How $p_i$ and $f_i$ are decided depends on the actual task we want to complete.

Distributed optimization can have different settings. Here we provide some classic examples. For example, distributed training is one of the most common ways to train a neural network, such as a foundation model. Apart from training models in data centers, people have also developed gossip-based P2P decentralized training for geographically distributed LLM pretraining. Federated learning is another form of distributed optimization in which data are kept locally.

## Multi-Agent Reaosning
LLM-powered agents are increasingly deployed for complex, multi-step tasks. Recently, people beging to explore the opportunity that how we can let multiple agents collaborate with each other sovling one complex tasks or multiple different tasks at the same time. In multi-agent collaboration, the canonical paradigm is at inference level, where the text is the most communication modal. Agents exchange the inference output with each other to complete one task together or collaboratr with each other.

There are different applications of multi-agent reaosnoing. One common application is that mutli-agents collaborate with each other to resolve one compex problems through different communication protocols and collarabotion strategies such as [proving Riemann Hypothesis](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf), [rigidifying a floppy moleculey](https://deepmind.google/blog/agentic-cheminformatics-with-google-antigravity/), and [solving Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/). Also, similar to federated learning and distributed training, multiple agent systems can work on different own tasks of their own domains and communicate with each other to share insights and improve each individual agent such as Federation over Text <d-cite key="yao2026federation"></d-cite>, federated agent optimization <d-cite key="yang2026federated"></d-cite>, and Dynamic Agent Skills <d-cite key="li2026dynamic"></d-cite>. Multiple agents can also interact in a decentralized way like a research town <d-cite key="liu2023dynamic,chen2024agentverse,yu2024researchtown"></d-cite>. We summarize that for multi-agent settings, we can use agents of the same LLM or different LLMs to solve one single tasks or solve individual task respectively.

Nevertheless, although we can use a general form $\max_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{A})$ to notate the objective of maxmizing rewards with agents, there is no clear optimization form we can directly work on to optimize. Hence, this raises the challenge that when we meet various [challenges](#shared-common-challenges) in multi-agent collaboration, it is harder for us to optimize like distributed optimization. As a result, in this post, we are going to try to draw a connection between distributed optimization to multi-agent reaosning so that we when deal with multi-agent reaosnoing, we can dervie the optimization ideas into new agent scenario and make the problems easier to resolve.

# Shared Common Challenges
We next describe three of the core shared challenges associated with solving the distributed optimization problem and multi-agent reaosning. From these challenges, we can conceptually see that how ideas in solving challenges in distirbuted optimziation and enlighten solving challenges in multi-agent reaosning.

## Performance
The first challenge is how we can improve the performance, which is accuracy in major cases. If we give a distribtued system the same amounts of data, the challenge is how we can make the downstream users to complete their tasks the best.

| Challenges | Distributed Optimization | Multi-Agent Collaboration |
|:-----------|:-------------------------|:----------------------|
|Aggregation| How we can aggregate model weights or gradients distributed over devices together?|How we can coorodinate agent output reasoning togerther for a good final answer?|
|Data Distribution|Each distributed worker can hold different numbers of samples or the data are non identical independent distributed | Each distribtued agent can hold different numbers of task and these tasks can from different domains and types (coding, question answering, or computer use agent tasks).|
|Personalization|Different clients may want to hold different local models rather than a global model and achieve different objectives|Different agents may want to hold a different memory and harness and resolve different types of tasks.|
{: .do2ma-challenges}

## Efficiency
Along with performance, there are many challenges bottleneck the effieincy in both scenarios. There are not only stastic level effieicny such as the convergence but also the system efficiency such as the wall-clock time needed for training or inference.

| Challenges | Distributed Optimization | Multi-Agent Collaboration |
|:-----------|:-------------------------|:----------------------|
| Statistical Heterogeneity| How do we dervie the guaranteed theoretical bound for convergence? |How do we decide how many rounds or iterations that multiple agents need to communicate with each other and stop?|
| Systems Heterogeneity| Different devices can have different variability in hardware (CPU, GPU, memory), network connectivity (NVlink, PCIe, Wifi)| Different agents can have different harware, network connecitivty, and token budgets|
|Expensive Communication| Frequent exchange of model weights or gradients between workers will cause heavy communication load| Excessive exchange of long model reaosning outputs and chain of thoughts will cause heavy communication load|
{: .do2ma-challenges}

## Trustworthiness
The thid important challenge in a common distributed system would be the trustworthiness where how the results distributed systems generate is reliable and trustyworhty. More importantly, it has been a long-term challenge that how we can preserve the privacy of data on distribtued workers and how we can make sure the whole distribtued system is sufficienctly robust, safe, and secure to various vulneraibilities and potential malicious attacks.

| Challenges | Distributed Optimization | Multi-Agent Collaboration |
|:-----------|:-------------------------|:----------------------|
|Robustness| How do we guarantee the correctness if some workers are malicious and submit sub-optimal model weights and gradients? |How do we guarantee the correctness of skills and reaosnoing if some workers are malicious and submit faulty chain-of-thoughts or reasoning trajectories?|
|Safety and Security|How do we handle the case that one or several colluding clients work together to break and corrupt the system?|How do deal with the vulneraibilty that one or several colluding agents work together to exploit and break the whole system?|
|Privacy| How do we preserve the sensitive information of the each worker's local data? |How do we make sure that the sensitive information in the prompt, task description, and task environment will not be exposed to other agents?|
{: .do2ma-challenges}

In summary, we can see that both distributed optimziation and multi-agent collaboration share important challenges where two schemas share similar high-level ideas behind.
